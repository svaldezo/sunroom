# Flotation Protocol for Archaeobotanical Samples

Flotation is the technique used to separate carbonized plant remains from excavated sediment. The method exploits the density difference between charred material and soil.

## Equipment

The setup requires a flotation tank, two mesh sieves, a water pump, and drying screens. The light fraction mesh must be finer than 0.5 millimeters or small seeds will pass through and be lost.

## Procedure

1. Record the sample bag number and the provenience before opening the bag.
2. Weigh the dry sediment and log the mass in the sample register.
3. Pour the sediment slowly into the agitating water column.
4. Skim the floating light fraction into the fine mesh sieve.
5. Rinse the heavy fraction that settles at the bottom of the tank.
6. Dry both fractions in shade for at least 48 hours.
7. Sort the dried light fraction under a low-power stereoscopic microscope.

Pouring the sediment too quickly causes turbulence, and turbulence drives light material into the heavy fraction. That loss is not recoverable, because the operator cannot tell afterward which seeds were driven down.

## Common failures

Drying in direct sunlight makes carbonized seeds brittle. Brittle seeds fracture during sorting, which destroys the diagnostic features used for identification. Roughly 15 percent of samples processed before 2019 show this damage.

Contamination between samples occurs when the tank is not fully drained. Always drain and wipe the tank between proveniences.
