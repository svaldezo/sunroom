# Structure and Agency

## The problem

Social theory has long struggled with a tension between structure and agency. Structure refers to the patterned arrangements that constrain what people can do. Agency refers to the capacity of individuals to act independently of those arrangements.

Neither concept is satisfying alone. A purely structural account cannot explain change, because if arrangements fully determine action then nothing would ever differ. A purely agentive account cannot explain regularity, because if individuals were unconstrained their behavior would not form patterns.

## Structuration

Structuration is the proposition that structure and agency are mutually constitutive rather than opposed. Structure is both the medium and the outcome of the practices it recursively organizes.

This claim is difficult because it refuses the usual causal ordering. Structure does not precede action in time. It exists only in its instantiation, which means it exists only insofar as people keep enacting it.

## Practice

Habitus denotes the durable dispositions through which people generate practices without following explicit rules. The dispositions are acquired, not chosen, and they feel to their bearer like preference rather than constraint.

Because habitus is acquired within a particular position, it tends to reproduce the conditions of that position. This is why inequality persists without anyone deciding that it should.

Doxa is the set of assumptions so taken for granted that they are not experienced as assumptions at all. Doxa is invisible to those who hold it, which is what distinguishes it from opinion.
