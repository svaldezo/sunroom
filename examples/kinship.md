# Kinship and Obligation

Kinship is a system that organizes obligation between people. It is not merely a record of biological descent, and treating it as a genealogy chart misses what it actually does.

## How delayed exchange works

First, a household produces a surplus of food beyond what it needs for the season. Then, that surplus is distributed outward to kin living outside the household. Finally, the receiving household is bound to reciprocate at some later date, and the timing of that return is deliberately left open.

Because reciprocity is delayed rather than immediate, the obligation persists across seasons and binds the two households together. Roughly 60 percent of surveyed households reported carrying at least one outstanding obligation.

## An example from the Trobriands

Among the Trobriand Islanders, yam gardens are planted by a man for his sister's husband. The yams are stacked in a display house where everyone can see them, so the debt is public.

Reciprocity is the expectation that a gift creates a counter-obligation in the receiver. This expectation does not require a contract, and it does not require trust in the individual, because the whole village observes the exchange.
